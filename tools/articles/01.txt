A: German aircraft and U-boats had been laying a new weapon in British shipping lanes: a magnetic mine that sat on the seabed and fired when a steel hull passed overhead. Losses spiked in November, including the Dutch liner Simon Bolivar off Harwich with heavy loss of life, and the Thames estuary was at times almost closed. On the night of 22–23 November a mine dropped by parachute landed on mudflats at Shoeburyness. Two Royal Navy officers from HMS Vernon dismantled it by hand and worked out the mechanism. Within weeks the Admiralty had countermeasures: "degaussing" cables wrapped around hulls to neutralise their magnetism and minesweeping techniques that could set the mines off safely. It was an early example of the technical war that would run in parallel with the fighting.
