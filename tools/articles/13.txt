On 11 April American troops liberated the concentration camp at Buchenwald, and on 15 April British troops entered Bergen-Belsen, where they found tens of thousands of sick and dying prisoners.

A: Early on 16 April Zhukov's First Belorussian Front opened the Berlin offensive with one of the heaviest artillery bombardments in history from its Oder bridgehead at Küstrin. Searchlights were turned on the German positions to blind the defenders; instead they lit up the Soviet troops in the smoke. The German defenders on the Seelow Heights, having pulled back before the bombardment, held for three days, inflicting heavy losses. To the south, Konev's First Ukrainian Front crossed the Neisse river and made faster progress, and Stalin let him turn north toward Berlin, setting off a race between the two marshals. It was the last great battle of the war in Europe.

A: On 1 April the US First and Ninth Armies had met at Lippstadt, encircling Field Marshal Walter Model's Army Group B in the Ruhr industrial region. The pocket was compressed through mid-April, and on 18 April resistance ended. Over 300,000 German soldiers were taken prisoner. Model dissolved his army group and on 21 April shot himself in a forest near Duisburg. American forces reached the Elbe at Magdeburg on 11 April, but Eisenhower had decided to leave Berlin to the Soviets. British troops reached the outskirts of Bremen and Hamburg. In Italy, the final Allied offensive had broken through the German lines.

A: On 20 April, Hitler's 56th birthday, Soviet artillery began to shell Berlin. By 25 April Soviet forces had encircled the city. Inside, the defence relied on remnants of army units, SS, Volkssturm and Hitler Youth. On 25 April American and Soviet troops met on the Elbe at Torgau and Strehla, cutting Germany in two; the meeting was celebrated with handshakes, toasts and photographs. In Italy, partisans rose in Milan, Turin and Genoa. On 28 April Mussolini, caught by partisans while trying to escape to Switzerland, was shot with his mistress Clara Petacci; their bodies were displayed hanging in a Milan square.

A: On 30 April, with Soviet troops a few hundred metres away, Hitler shot himself in his bunker under the Reich Chancellery; his wife, Eva Braun, whom he had married the day before, took poison. Their bodies were burned in the garden. That evening Soviet soldiers raised a flag over the Reichstag; the famous photograph was restaged on 2 May. Berlin's garrison surrendered on 2 May, the same day German forces in Italy surrendered. Admiral Dönitz, named Hitler's successor, tried to delay surrender so that as many Germans as possible could reach the Western Allies. Germany surrendered unconditionally at Reims on 7 May and in Berlin on 8 May. The war in Europe was over.
