H: A state of siege in Moscow

A: On 19 October Stalin declared a state of siege in Moscow. Hundreds of thousands of civilians, most of them women, dug anti-tank ditches and trenches around the city. Militia divisions of factory workers and students were thrown into the line with little training. Fresh divisions were beginning to arrive from Siberia and the Far East, released after the spy Richard Sorge in Tokyo reported that Japan intended to strike south rather than north; Stalin had other sources too. In the south, German forces took Kharkov on 24 October and broke into Crimea, laying siege to Sevastopol. The German advance on Moscow had slowed to a crawl in the mud.

A: By the end of October the mud had brought Typhoon to a halt. Vehicles sank to their axles; tanks used up fuel at several times the normal rate; horses died in harness. In the Atlantic, a U-boat sank the US destroyer Reuben James on 31 October, killing over a hundred American sailors.

@@ 115 | 55.75,37.62 | Red Square, Moscow | east

A: On 7 November, the anniversary of the revolution, Stalin held the traditional military parade on Red Square, with troops marching past the Kremlin walls and straight on to the front, some 70 kilometres away. Snow fell during the parade. The night before, in a speech held in the Mayakovskaya metro station, he had declared that if the Germans wanted a war of extermination, they would get one. Newsreels of the parade went around the world as proof that Moscow still stood. The Red Army had lost several million men in five months, and Stalin was ordering a vast mobilisation to replace them, conscripting older men and training new divisions in the east.

A: On the night of 8–9 November Force K, two cruisers and two destroyers based at Malta, used radar to find and destroy an entire Italian convoy carrying supplies to Rommel, the "Duisburg convoy", without loss. The Germans responded by sending U-boats into the Mediterranean and transferring a Luftwaffe air corps from Russia to Sicily. On 13 November a U-boat sank the carrier Ark Royal near Gibraltar.

@@ 117 | 31.72,24.50 | Sidi Rezegh, Libya | africa

A: On 18 November the newly named British Eighth Army, under Alan Cunningham, launched Operation Crusader to destroy Rommel's armour and relieve Tobruk. It was the largest British offensive of the war so far, with over 700 tanks. The tank battles around the airfield at Sidi Rezegh, south-east of Tobruk, were chaotic and swung back and forth. British armour was committed piecemeal and suffered heavily against German anti-tank guns. On the night before the offensive, a British commando raid aimed at killing or capturing Rommel at what was thought to be his headquarters failed. On the Eastern Front, the ground had frozen and Typhoon resumed.

A: With the ground hard, Army Group Centre attacked again in mid-November in a pincer around Moscow from the north and south. In Libya, Rommel launched a deep raid toward the Egyptian frontier.

A: The German attack stalled in the first days of December at temperatures of minus 30 and below. On 5 December, Soviet forces north of Moscow struck back, and the next day the counteroffensive widened along the whole front. German commanders, exhausted and freezing, asked to retreat. Hitler forbade it, then dismissed generals who disobeyed. The German army had suffered its first major defeat. On 7 December, Japanese aircraft attacked the US Pacific Fleet at Pearl Harbor and Japanese forces landed in Malaya; the United States declared war on Japan on the 8th, and Britain did the same. The war was now global.

@@ 120k | 52.52,13.38 | Reichstag, Berlin | europe | sober
H: Germany declares war on the United States
P: f:Bundesarchiv Bild 183-1987-0703-014, Berlin, Reichstag, Sitzung.jpg ; s:Reichstag 11 December 1941 Hitler speech
A: On 11 December Hitler told the Reichstag that Germany was at war with the United States; Italy followed the same day. He was not obliged to by the Tripartite Pact, since Japan had attacked first. He seems to have believed war with America was coming anyway and that U-boats could now be unleashed on American shipping. It was one of his most consequential decisions. On 12 December he met Nazi party leaders, and according to Goebbels's diary he spoke of the "annihilation" of the Jews as the necessary consequence of the world war he claimed they had caused. Historians see these weeks as a turning point in the decision to murder all of Europe's Jews. Mass shootings in the east were already under way; the first gassings at Chełmno began on 8 December.

A: In the days after Pearl Harbor Japanese forces struck across the Pacific and South-East Asia. In Russia, the Soviet counteroffensive pushed Army Group Centre back from Moscow; Kalinin was retaken on 16 December. Within a fortnight the Western position in Asia was falling apart.

A: Hong Kong's British, Canadian, Indian and local defenders held the island for nearly a week against Japanese assaults before surrendering on Christmas Day, "Black Christmas". A: On 1 January 1942 twenty-six nations signed the "Declaration by United Nations" in Washington, pledging to use their full resources against the Axis and not to make a separate peace. The name was Roosevelt's. The Arcadia Conference created the Combined Chiefs of Staff to direct the British and American war effort, and a unified command in South-East Asia under Wavell. Manila was declared an open city and occupied on 2 January; American and Filipino troops withdrew to the Bataan peninsula. In Libya, Crusader had forced Rommel back to El Agheila, where he had started in March; Tobruk had been relieved.

@@ 124 | 14.68,120.54 | Bataan | pacific

A: About 80,000 American and Filipino troops under Douglas MacArthur dug in on the Bataan peninsula, while MacArthur himself directed the defence from the island fortress of Corregidor. Those who were there called themselves the "battling bastards of Bataan".

@@ 125 | 40.70,-73.50 | Off New York | atlantic
H: U-boats off the American coast
P: w:Operation Drumbeat ; s:tanker torpedoed off US coast 1942
A: In mid-January five long-range U-boats arrived off the east coast of the United States in Operation Paukenschlag, Drumbeat. They found ships sailing alone with navigation lights on, coastal towns brightly lit behind them, and almost no convoy system or anti-submarine patrols. In the next months U-boats sank hundreds of ships in American waters, many of them tankers, often within sight of the shore. U-boat crews called it the "second happy time". The US Navy under Admiral King was slow to adopt convoys, partly for lack of escorts. It would take until the summer before losses were brought under control. Americans on beaches watched burning tankers at night.

@@ 126 | 52.43,13.17 | Wannsee, Berlin | europe | sober
H: The Wannsee Conference
P: f:Villa Wannsee - Haus der Wannsee-Konferenz.jpg ; w:Wannsee Conference ; s:Haus der Wannsee-Konferenz
A: On 20 January fifteen senior officials of the SS, Nazi party and government ministries met at a villa on Lake Wannsee in Berlin, chaired by Reinhard Heydrich. The meeting lasted about 90 minutes. Its purpose was to coordinate the "final solution of the Jewish question" across the bureaucracy: who would be included, how mixed-ancestry cases would be treated, and how Jews from all over Europe would be deported east. The minutes, prepared by Adolf Eichmann, listed some 11 million Jews to be included. Mass murder had already begun in the occupied USSR and at Chełmno; Wannsee organised its extension to the whole continent. The villa is now a memorial and education centre.
